# Candidate gate: database-state lineage and agent-evaluation uncertainty

Date: 2026-11-06  
Status: **HOLD. A concrete benchmark-structure signal is verified; no model-score effect or independent paper contribution is established.**

## Research question

> When multiple data-agent task IDs share the same initial database state, does treating task IDs as independent observations overstate the certainty of model comparisons that are meant to generalize to new data environments?

This concerns inference beyond the fixed benchmark score. DAComp-DE's equal-task-weighted score remains a valid descriptive score for its published finite task set. Repeated environment states only matter when the study interprets task-level outcomes as evidence about new environments, confidence intervals, or ranking certainty.

## Verified artifact evidence

**[Fact]** The public DAComp-DE archive at Hugging Face revision `8c4a18983b0733256fffb593f7febb9ab6697854` is 2,267,093,861 bytes. A range-only read of its 619,354-byte ZIP central directory found 4,990 entries and 30 `arch`, 50 `evol`, and 30 `impl` task directories. No full archive was downloaded.

**[Fact]** Across the 50 `evol` tasks, the archive metadata reports 30 distinct `(filename, CRC32, uncompressed-size)` signatures for initial databases and 30 for final databases. For each role, 20 signatures occur in two task IDs and 10 occur once. Across the 30 `impl` tasks there are 30 initial-database signatures. Nineteen initial-database signatures occur in both `evol` and `impl`, yielding 41 distinct signatures across the 80 implementation/evolution tasks.

**[Fact]** A follow-up range audit read and decompressed all 67 members belonging to the 28 repeated `(CRC32, uncompressed-size)` groups among these 80 start databases. Each member matched the archive's size and CRC, and every member within each repeated group had the same raw-file SHA-256. Therefore the 80 task IDs map to 41 exact starting-database states: 28 states are reused by 67 task IDs, and 13 states occur once. No database file was persisted. The audit read 373,718,692 compressed bytes, rather than downloading the 2.27 GB archive.

**[Exploratory]** The 50-row `evol` question index contains distinct prompts for all 20 same-signature pairs. A simple case-folded token-set Jaccard score ranges from 0.205 to 0.356 across those pairs. This rules out literal duplicate prompts in that quick screen, but it is not a semantic similarity measure and does not establish that outcomes are correlated.

**[Inference]** The task ID is not always the unique initial data-state unit in this archive. Exact state reuse creates a measurable lineage facet and a plausible within-dataset dependence structure. It does not, by itself, prove correlated model outcomes, invalid published scores, or an evaluation defect.

## Estimands and planned comparison

1. Preserve the official equal-task-weighted point estimate for the fixed benchmark.
2. For generalization to new environments, compare task-IID uncertainty with uncertainty that accounts for initial-state lineage. Keep the point estimand explicit; do not confuse equal-task weighting with equal-environment weighting.
3. Primary outcomes: paired model score difference, its interval, probability of each model ranking first, and whether a prespecified comparison changes its decision under lineage-aware uncertainty.
4. Report equal-state-weighted results only as a separate estimand. Construct conservative lineage clusters from exact start-state SHA-256; test broader source-system clusters only as a prespecified sensitivity analysis.

### Cluster-count design sensitivity (not outcome evidence)

The verified 80-task structure has 17 repeated-state clusters of size 2, 11 of size 3, and 13 singletons: 41 exact state clusters total. For an equal-task-weighted mean and an assumed exchangeable within-state outcome correlation `rho`, the standard design-effect sensitivity is `DE = 1 + rho * (sum_j m_j^2 / N - 1)`. Here `sum_j m_j^2 = 180`, so `DE = 1 + 1.25*rho` and the illustrative effective task count `N/DE` is 80 at `rho=0`, 61 at `rho=0.25`, 49 at `rho=0.50`, 41 at `rho=0.75`, and 36 at `rho=1`. For `evol` alone, the documented structure is 20 pairs plus 10 singletons (`N=50`, `K=30`), giving `DE = 1 + 0.8*rho` and effective counts 50, 42, 36, 31, and 28 at the same rho values.

These calculations are design sensitivity only: the ICC is unknown and has not been estimated; `N/DE` is not a power calculation and does not establish correlation. The number of exact environments is modest, so a one-model or one-run pilot cannot support a broad paper claim. Before execution, pre-register an outcome contrast, practical decision margin, repeated-run policy, and a rule for whether this 30/41-cluster design is pilot-only or adequate for a DAComp-scoped confirmatory result.

### Mean-difference precision screen (assumption-only)

To check whether all 80 tasks could even resolve a moderate *paired model-score difference*, an analytic sensitivity calculation assumed standardized task-level paired differences with marginal SD 1, an exchangeable random intercept within exact-state clusters, two-sided alpha .05, and a cluster degrees-of-freedom approximation of `G-1`. It uses `DE = 1 + rho * (sum(m_j^2)/N - 1)`, `N_eff=N/DE`, noncentrality `d*sqrt(N_eff)`, and a normal-tail power approximation with a `t_(.975,G-1)` critical value. Using the verified cluster-size histogram (13 singletons, 17 pairs, 11 triples), the approximate standardized difference needed for 80% power is 0.32, 0.37, 0.41, 0.45, and 0.48 SD at assumed within-state ICCs 0, .25, .50, .75, and 1. For the 50-task evol-only subset (10 singletons and 20 pairs; 30 clusters), the corresponding range is 0.41–0.55 SD. These are assumption-driven sensitivities for a mean difference only; they use no observed model variance, do not power the primary residual-dependence/decision-change hypothesis, and cannot justify an equivalence or null conclusion. Thus 80 tasks would still be a screening/pilot design unless the preregistered target is a large effect and the actual paired variance supports it.

## Near-neighbor boundary

- **DAComp, arXiv:2512.04324, Appendix A.1:** defines task-level CS/CFS/SR and reports the benchmark score as an average over tasks. The paper's metric definition does not establish that tasks are independent draws from new data environments.
- **DAComp, arXiv:2512.04324, §7.4:** already evaluates judge reliability at item, case, and model granularity; it uses case-level ICC(A,1) and model-level Kendall's tau-b to compare human and LLM-judge scores/rankings. The public leaderboard exposes category-level aggregates, and the paper's inspected results do not provide the complete per-task, per-model matrix needed to estimate the proposed initial-state effect. Therefore “agent-evaluation reliability,” “ranking uncertainty,” or “judge reliability” are not contribution claims available to this project. Any surviving increment must be the conditional empirical value of *exact database-state lineage beyond task-conditioned uncertainty*, not another generic reliability metric.
- **Agent Evaluation Reliability: More Tasks Won't (Always) Fix An Agent Leaderboard, arXiv:2610.00651:** uses generalizability theory and a Bayesian task/model/scaffold variance decomposition across agent benchmarks. It is a close methodological neighbor and may absorb most of a generic “cluster-aware uncertainty” claim. The inspected model indexes tasks within benchmarks but does not identify database-state lineage as a separate observed facet.
- **What Does an LLM-Agent Leaderboard Rank Actually Compare?, arXiv:2609.07785 (Wei-Jung Huang, 2026-09-07):** proposes estimand-aware pairwise conclusions, common-support checks, practical margins, and uncertainty using matched-task or grouped-cell resampling. It analyzes public leaderboard results, repeated attempts, and target sensitivity; it also explicitly limits analyses where task-level records or dependence structures are absent. It does not appear to model exact shared database-state lineage as a separate observed facet. This narrows the possible distinction, but means a paper on “task dependence changes leaderboard certainty” would overlap directly.
- Cluster-robust inference and hierarchical modeling are established statistical tools. Rebranding them is not a contribution. The candidate survives only if the data-state lineage is reproducible, materially changes an agent-evaluation conclusion, and offers evidence beyond a routine application of the neighboring framework.

### What is still potentially distinct—and what is not

- **Not a contribution by itself:** finding duplicate starting databases, substituting a cluster bootstrap for an IID bootstrap, or reporting wider intervals. These are a benchmark-specific application of established dependence-aware inference and are now especially close to the pairwise public-leaderboard work above.
- **Potential empirical increment:** demonstrate, on task-level outcomes from at least two independent data-agent benchmarks, that exact database-state lineage predicts within-cluster outcome dependence and changes a prespecified pairwise evaluation decision beyond task-level/grouped resampling and existing task/model/scaffold reliability analyses. The baseline must include the strongest available task-conditioned uncertainty analysis, not only IID bootstrap. DAComp's published judge-agreement analyses further rule out repackaging ICC, rank agreement, or generic evaluator reliability as the increment.
- **Required novelty test:** show that adding the observed state-lineage facet explains material residual dependence after the neighboring paper's task/benchmark conditioning, and that the resulting change is not merely a different target weighting. If the effect disappears after task-conditioned resampling, the candidate is NO-GO.

## Decision

**HOLD; no independent contribution established.** The source-structure claim is verified at the byte level: 80 `impl`/`evol` task IDs map to 41 exact starting-database states; 28 states are reused across 67 task IDs, and 13 occur once. Shared-state `evol` pairs have distinct prompts in a lexical screen. Generic task clustering, leaderboard uncertainty, and judge-reliability claims are already close to or directly covered by existing methods and by DAComp's own ICC/rank-agreement evaluation. Keep this candidate only as an empirical question about whether exact database-state lineage adds information beyond those baselines and changes a prespecified comparison. No task-outcome matrix has been analyzed, so there is no evidence of residual dependence or a changed decision. Next gate: obtain per-task outcomes from a fixed, reproducible harness and compare against the strongest task-conditioned baseline; first resolve the missing runtime and safe-isolation route. If the state facet adds no residual signal or decision change, retire the candidate rather than expanding documentation around it.

**[Fact] Full application/state crosswalk.** `da_comp_app_state_crosswalk.py` joined all 80 starting-database archive entries at the pinned revision to the existing byte-SHA-256 hashes for repeated signatures. It wrote [DAComp_APP_STATE_CROSSWALK_2026_11_06.csv](DAComp_APP_STATE_CROSSWALK_2026_11_06.csv). Repeated signatures use the verified raw database SHA-256; singleton signatures are unique `(CRC32, uncompressed size)` pairs in the complete archive. Because byte-identical files must share that pair, the singleton entries cannot duplicate another archived state. The crosswalk contains 80 task rows, 41 distinct state clusters, and 29 application families.

**[Fact] Within-application variation is concentrated.** Twelve of the 29 application families contain multiple initial states. In nine, two evolution tasks share one state while one implementation task has a different singleton state. Salesforce has six tasks split into two three-task state groups, each containing two evolution tasks and one implementation task. Qualtrics and Workday each have one implementation and one evolution task on two different singleton states, so neither supplies a repeated-state pair. The other 17 application families map all their task IDs to one initial state. Seventeen of those single-state families have a repeated state spanning both task types; that is shared-state coverage, not a within-family contrast between alternative states.

**[Inference / identifiability consequence]** `da_comp_state_identifiability.py` creates indicator columns for 58 non-empty application-family×task-type cells. The baseline rank is 58; adding all 41 state-cluster indicators raises it to 59 (one incremental degree of freedom), entirely from the contrast between the two Salesforce state clusters. The 13 singleton states each occur in a one-task app/type cell and are therefore aliased with that baseline. The rank audit is recorded in [DAComp_STATE_IDENTIFIABILITY_2026_11_06.json](DAComp_STATE_IDENTIFIABILITY_2026_11_06.json); its input crosswalk SHA-256 is included there. This is a design-matrix result, not an outcome or variance estimate. The 9 same-state Evol pairs lack a same-application, same-type alternative-state contrast; the other shared states are constant within their application×type cells and are absorbed by app/type controls. Salesforce's single additional contrast also compares different task IDs, so task-content and state cannot be causally separated by this benchmark alone. A task-IID versus state-cluster interval comparison would overstate support for a general state-specific effect.
